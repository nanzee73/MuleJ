MuleJ – User guide

MuleJ turns any picture into C64 graphics: a multicolor picture (Koala Painter), a hires picture (Art Studio) or a PETSCII text screen (Petmate) – each also as a runnable C64 program. It checks that every cell keeps the C64 color rules and can fix the cells that don't. Everything also works on the command line.

Quick start

  1. Choose the Target at the top: Multicolor, Hires or PETSCII.
  2. Open picture… or drop a file onto the window. MuleJ looks at the picture and decides where to start (see Start at).
  3. Go through the steps with Next ▶ and ◀ Back, or click a step in the bar at the top. Every step shows its input on the left and its output on the right.
  4. In the last step, Result, the colour check must pass. Then save the C64 file (.kla, .art or Petmate) and/or a runnable PRG.

The chain of steps

The two previews show the input and the output of the current step; the settings of the step sit below them. Drag the divider between previews and settings to change the sizes – it is set to fit whenever you change the step.

1 Original → 2 Frame → 3 Prepare → 4 Pixelizer (multicolor only) → 5 Colours → 6 Result. Each step works on the output of the step before. Change something in an early step and all later steps update automatically. A step that is "skipped" passes the picture through unchanged; Use this step switches it on again.

Opening pictures and "Start at"

MuleJ reads every common PC format (PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP, WebP, TIFF, …) and C64 pictures: Koala, Art Studio, Advanced Art Studio, Drazpaint, Drazlace, FLI, FLI Graph, AFLI, Gunpaint, Funpaint, NUFLI, MUFLI, MUIFLI and FlipperJ's raw files. C64 pictures are drawn with the selected palette; Art Studio, AFLI, NUFLI, MUFLI and MUIFLI switch the target to Hires, the others to Multicolor.

Start at decides which steps the picture still needs. Automatic looks at the picture:

  • not 320×200 → start at Frame;
  • 320×200 with free colours → start at Prepare;
  • C64 colours but pixel pairs differ (multicolor) → start at Pixelizer;
  • C64 size and C64 colours → start at the colour check (Result), nothing is changed.

Choose a step by hand if your picture was already prepared or edited elsewhere. All steps before it are passed through unchanged.

Step 2: Frame

Choose the 320×200 part of the picture: drag inside the frame to move it, drag the corner to resize it, drag outside to draw a new one; mouse wheel = zoom, right or middle drag = pan, double click = whole picture.

  • Aspect ratio of the result (C64 PAL, square pixels, NTSC or free) and Lock frame to target aspect.
  • Scaling: Stretch, Fit (with borders, see Border colour…), Fill (crop) or 1:1. 320×200 frame (1:1) takes exactly 320×200 pixels without scaling – a click moves the frame there.
  • Filter: Area average (smooth) for photos, Nearest neighbour for pixel art.
  • Whole picture and Max. centered frame are shortcuts; X, Y, Width and Height set the frame exactly.

Step 3: Prepare

Contrast and Brightness (-100 to 100), Gamma % (below 100 = brighter middle tones) and Saturation % (0 = grey). The C64 has few dark and few saturated colours, so a little more contrast and saturation often helps.

Step 4: Pixelizer (multicolor only)

Multicolor pixels are twice as wide, so every pair of pixels becomes one. Which pixel survives decides how thin lines look:

  • Left → right: the left pixel of every pair is kept.
  • Right → left: the right pixel is kept.
  • Average: both are mixed – softer, good for photos.

Step 5: Colours

  • Palette: which RGB colours the 16 C64 colours have (Colodore, Pepto, DeeKay, …). Custom… opens the palette editor: change colours, load .vpl/.gpl/hex files, save as .vpl.
  • Matching: how "similar" is measured – RGB euclidean (as the old c64palettizer), RGB weighted, YUV or HSL.
  • Dithering (Floyd-Steinberg, Atkinson, Jarvis, Stucki, Sierra Lite, Bayer 2×2/4×4/8×8) mixes colours in fine patterns. Strength sets how much; Serpentine scanning avoids diagonal patterns.
  • Bleeding (across cells): the dither error may continue into neighbouring cells – smoother, but cell edges are less clean.
  • Background: automatic (best colour) or fixed (multicolor and PETSCII).
  • Correct automatically: cells with too many colours get the colours that reproduce them best. With dithering the result always keeps the rules.

PETSCII settings

Each cell gets the character and colour that look most like the original – rated pixel by pixel and blurred, as the eye sees it.

  • Characters: Upper case + graphics or Lower + upper case (the two C64 character sets).
  • Use letters and digits: off = only graphics characters, space and reversed space – usually calmer.
  • Detail: high = characters follow edges pixel by pixel; low = only brightness and colour of the cells (soft).
  • Diffusion: how much of a cell's remaining error is passed on to its neighbours (good for gradients and areas).
  • Calm: busy characters only appear where they clearly help.

To edit a PETSCII picture further, save it as a Petmate file and open it in Petmate.

Step 6: Result – colour check, correction, editing

MuleJ checks all 1000 cells. "✔" means every cell keeps the rules; "✘" shows how many cells have too many colours.

  • ◀ ▶ jump to the previous / next cell with too many colours. Mark shows them in the preview: red = breaks the rules, yellow = corrected, cyan = selected cell (only in the preview, never in files).
  • Click into result: Select cell – a left click selects a cell and shows its colours with the number of pixels; Paint – a left click or drag sets pixels to the chosen colour.
  • For a selected cell: press up to the allowed number of colours (BG = background, always allowed in multicolor) and Apply colours – every pixel gets the nearest of them. Automatic chooses the best colours for this cell, Reset cell discards your colours and painted pixels, Discard all does so for every cell (after asking).
  • Fix all gives every cell with too many colours the colours that reproduce it best – like Automatic for each conflict cell at once. Afterwards every cell can still be changed or reset by hand.

Saving

  • Save Koala (.kla)… / Save Art Studio (.art)… / Save Petmate… – the C64 file, only once the colour check has passed. It is suggested in the folder of the source picture.
  • Save PRG… – a runnable C64 program that shows the picture: LOAD"NAME",8 and RUN; any key ends it.
  • This step as PNG… – the output of the shown step without markers, e.g. to edit it on the PC or phone and load it again later (choose that step in "Start at"). The enlargement factor next to it makes editing easier; MuleJ reduces the picture exactly again when loading.
  • MuleJ asks before overwriting a file, and warns in particular before overwriting the picture you opened.

Previews, zoom and pan

  • Mouse wheel = zoom, right drag = pan, double click = fit. Buttons: −, +, Fit and 4× (4 screen pixels per picture pixel).
  • Link zooms and pans both previews together, Grid shows pixel and 8×8 cell lines when zoomed in, PAL shows 320×200 pictures with the aspect ratio of a PAL monitor.
  • Every group has a Defaults button. Sliders: arrows move by 1, the mark shows the default, double click resets.
  • Language: menu Language. System language (the default) follows your computer – English if it is neither English nor German; a language chosen by hand is kept for the next start.

Command line

mulej <command> [options] <input> [output]
CommandDoes
palettizemaps all colours to the nearest palette colour → NAME_palettized.png
pixelizemakes 2×1 multicolor pixels → NAME_pixelized_l2r.png / _r2l.png
koalamulticolor bitmap → NAME.kla
hireshires bitmap → NAME.art
petsciitext screen → NAME.petmate
checklists the cells that break the rules of --mode mc|hires
topngC64 picture or Petmate file → NAME.png

Important options: --palette NAME|FILE, --space euclid|rgb|yuv|hsl, --fit stretch|fit|fill|none, --dither NAME, --strength N, --serpentine, --no-bleeding, --bg auto|0..15, --fix-clashes, --preview FILE.png, --prg FILE.prg, --contrast/--brightness/--gamma/--saturation N, --charset upper|lower, --text, --detail/--diffusion/--calm N. mulej --help lists all of them. The old -if FILE syntax of c64palettizer, c64pixelizer and c64koalaverter still works.

Tips

  • Pixel art drawn for the C64: start at the colour check, use no dithering and fix single cells by hand.
  • Photos: Average in the pixelizer, a little more contrast, Floyd-Steinberg or Atkinson dithering.
  • Too many conflicts: switch on Correct automatically or try a fixed background colour.
